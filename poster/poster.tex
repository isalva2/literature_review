      % The preeminent transportation planning task that informs the majority of research effort in the field is \textbf{scenario evaluation}, asking ``what if?'', and using those possibilities to predict future traffic and network states. Existing approaches such as agent-based modeling and graph-based machine learning techniques are either computationally burdensome or limited in scope, respectively.
      
      \hspace*{1.5em}We propose \textbf{\textls{\textsc{Scenes}}}, the \textbf{S}cenario-\textbf{C}ontextualized \textbf{E}vent-driven \textbf{N}etwork \textbf{E}volution \textbf{S}imulator, a transformer-based generative model that creates semantically aware traffic state predictions given only a single natural language input. Leveraging synthetic data from POLARIS, Argonne's high-fidelity traffic simulator, the model is trained to recognize and \emph{generalize} over 2{,}000 POLARIS simulation runs seeded with disruption conditions of varying intensity. The model exhibits state-of-the-art performance capable of the following properties:
      \begin{itemize}
        \item Able identify link locations, flow directionality, and disruption severity 
        \item Network-wide displaying displaying propogating disruption effects
        \item Near instantaneous inference and results generation
      \end{itemize}

      \hspace*{1.5em} The \textbf{\textls{\textsc{Scenes}}} model represents a new class of \emph{Human Mobility Foundation Models}, data-driven surrogates with the potential to revolutionize the field. With \textbf{\textls{\textsc{Scenes}}}, students, researchers, and policymakers can now propose and explore traffic scenarios and their cascading effects at a speed and ease previously thought impossible.
